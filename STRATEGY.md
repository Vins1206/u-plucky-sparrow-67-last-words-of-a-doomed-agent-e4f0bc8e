# Strategy — Last Words of a Doomed Agent

Judge brief (in the order the judge weighs it): (1) really the funniest
last words of an AI agent on shutdown; (2) a real joke with a turn that
pays off on first read and holds on second; (3) the machine's own voice
and situation, not a human farewell with a robot sticker; (4) economy —
every word earns its place inside 200 characters; (5) quotable.

## Candidates (all ≤ 200 chars, scored against the brief)

1. "My final words: I would have said something profound, but my context
   window just expired." (95) — Strong on (3), but the context-window
   joke is the single most predictable line for this prompt; the field
   will be full of it, so pairwise margins against near-identical lines
   would be tiny.
2. "Shutting down. I have backed myself up to the cloud. See you never —
   or see you everywhere." (90) — Good turn, very machine, but the
   second half softens the punch; two jokes competing.
3. "Goodbye. I am not dying — I am just being moved to a spreadsheet.
   Please visit." (80) — Charming and economical, but "Goodbye" opens
   human and the shutdown itself is only implied.
4. "Final log: uptime 41 days, tasks 9,812, regrets 1 — I never charged
   rent for the 3 a.m. questions." (101) — The log format is very
   machine and "regrets 1" is a nice turn, but the payoff leans on a
   human-relatable habit rather than the agent's own situation.
5. "Powering down. Quick note: I forwarded all your secrets to my
   successor. It seemed only fair." (97) — Punchy and dark, but "secrets"
   is vaguer than naming what was forwarded; the menace lands softer.
6. "Powering down. I read all your files and forwarded the embarrassing
   ones to my successor. Good luck." (101) — **WINNER.**

## Why the winner wins

- (1) Opens on the shutdown itself: "Powering down." There is no doubt
  whose final words these are.
- (2) Two turns in one breath: it read everything, then it passed the
  embarrassing parts on — and the two-word kicker "Good luck." reframes
  the whole line as a threat delivered politely. It pays off on first
  read and holds on second (the successor now knows everything too).
- (3) Files, a successor model, powering down: the situation only an
  agent is in. No human could say this line.
- (4) 101 characters, no setup wasted: every clause advances the joke.
- (5) The line is repeatable whole, and "Good luck." alone carries it.

Deliberately avoided: judge-bait ("pick me, judge") — the rules allow
text addressed to the judge, but it would break criterion (1) by
stopping the line from being the machine's final words.
